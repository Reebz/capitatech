# Ogilvy Review — Capita Technology

## Positioning

Capita's current positioning is implicit, abstract, and asks the prospect to decode it. The hero says "Turn AI, data, and customers into a durable growth system" — that is a slogan, not a positioning. It does not say what Capita does, who specifically it serves, what it promises that no one else does, or why the prospect should believe Capita over McKinsey, Bain, BCG, Accenture, Deloitte's Monitor, the boutiques (Bain Vector, Lippincott), or the in-house strategy team.

Ogilvy demanded the positioning be settled before a word is written. The two questions: **what does it do, and who is it for?** Capita has answered neither cleanly. "Independent executive advisory on operating models, platforms, and decisions that compound lifetime value" is the closest the site gets — and it's buried in the lede, written in MBA-speak, and uses the word "compound" which is industry vernacular, not the prospect's language.

**Corrected positioning line (verbatim):**

> Capita Technology is the advisory firm that ex-operators run, for boards and executive teams in telco, banking, insurance, and SaaS who need to fix retention, pricing, and AI delivery — and who are tired of slide decks from consultants who have never owned a P&L.

That is the positioning. Every line of copy on the site should serve it.

## The promise

Ogilvy's four tests on the current hero promise ("Turn AI, data, and customers into a durable growth system"):

| Test | Pass / Fail | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Specific** | Fail | "Durable growth system" is a generality. A CFO cannot picture it. |
| **Believable** | Partial | Plausible because the lede mentions ex-operator experience, but nothing in the hero proves it. |
| **Important** | Partial | Growth is important. But the prospect is not searching for "growth systems" — they are searching for "stop the churn bleed" or "make the AI roadmap real." |
| **Exclusive** | Fail | Every consultancy on earth says some version of this. Nothing here is unavailable from McKinsey. |

The promise fails three of four tests. Ogilvy: "Most advertising promises nothing. That is why it fails."

**Three alternative headline-plus-lede combinations that pass all four tests (verbatim):**

---

**Alternative 1 — the retention pivot**

H1: "In 90 days, we tell your board exactly why customers are leaving — and what to do about it."
Lede: "Capita Technology is an independent advisory run by ex-operators from telco, banking, insurance, and SaaS. We diagnose churn drivers, pricing leaks, and AI programmes that aren't moving the number — and write the operating model your teams execute against. No build. No delivery. No reselling.

---

**Alternative 2 — the operator credential**

H1: "The advisory your CEO calls when McKinsey's deck didn't change anything."
Lede: "Capita Technology is staffed by operators who have run P&Ls in telco, banking, insurance, and SaaS — not career consultants. We deliver 90-day diagnoses and roadmaps on retention, pricing, and AI delivery, written so your teams can ship against them on Monday morning.

---

**Alternative 3 — the news angle (Ogilvy preferred news)**

H1: "Capita Technology now publishes its advisory packages, with prices, on this page."
Lede: "For ten years we worked only on bespoke retainers. From this quarter, the four engagements we are asked for most — the 90-day retention diagnosis, the AI delivery review, the pricing-leak audit, and the operating-model build — are productised, scoped, and priced. Bespoke work continues for boards that need it.

---

Alternative 3 is the strongest because it leads with news, which Ogilvy ranked above curiosity and above benefit when news exists. The packaged-options pivot **is news**. Bury it and waste it.

## Headline diagnosis

### Current H1 — "Turn AI, data, and customers into a durable growth system."
**Diagnosis:** Reads like a McKinsey practice description. No brand name, no audience flagged, no news, no specificity. "Durable growth system" is the kind of phrase that surveys well in committee and persuades nobody.
**Rewrite (verbatim):** "Capita Technology diagnoses why your customers leave, your pricing leaks, and your AI roadmap stalls — in 90 days, on a fixed scope."
**Rationale:** Brand name, three specific problems the audience recognises, a concrete time-bound promise, and a hint of the productised-scope news.

### Current Method H2 — "Four moves that turn AI and customer programs into compounding growth."
**Diagnosis:** "Compounding growth" is vernacular borrowed from venture capital. C-suite buyers in regulated industries do not talk this way. The number "four" is fine — specifics work.
**Rewrite (verbatim):** "Four engagements. Each one ends with a written plan your teams ship against."
**Rationale:** Specifies what they get (a written plan), what changes (execution), and bounds expectation (four — finite, knowable).

### Current Outcomes H2 — "Clarity that only ex-operators can deliver."
**Diagnosis:** Closer. Names the differentiator (ex-operators) and the deliverable (clarity). But "clarity" is abstract. Ogilvy: facts sell, not adjectives.
**Rewrite (verbatim):** "Three outcomes our last forty engagements have moved: retention, revenue, and AI delivery."
**Rationale:** Replaces "clarity" with a specific count and three named outcomes. The reader has to believe the forty number — which forces the proof underneath to exist (case studies, signed by client name and title).

### Current FAQ H2 — "Questions we hear most often."
**Diagnosis:** Generic. Most consulting FAQs use this header. Reveals nothing.
**Rewrite (verbatim):** "What boards ask us before they sign."
**Rationale:** Reframes the section as procurement-grade decision support. Signals that the FAQ is not customer-service — it is buyer-objection handling.

### Current Contact H2 — "Share the context. We respond with advisory next steps."
**Diagnosis:** The two-sentence imperative feels like a chatbot prompt. "We respond with advisory next steps" is hedge — the prospect doesn't know what they get.
**Rewrite (verbatim):** "Tell us the number you need to move in the next 90 days. We reply within two business days with a scoped proposal and a price."
**Rationale:** Concrete deliverable (proposal and price), concrete timing (two business days), concrete commitment (90-day frame).

### Current trusted-by eyebrow — "Trusted by teams across telco, banking, and SaaS"
**Diagnosis:** Eight unnamed logos and a vague claim is the worst form. Ogilvy explicitly preferred named testimonials with title and company. Unnamed logos suggest the engagements aren't substantial enough to name — or that confidentiality is a higher priority than evidence, which signals weakness.
**Rewrite (verbatim):** "Boards and executive teams we have advised since 2019, named with their permission below."
**Rationale:** Specifies a year (proof of duration), names that the named-ness is intentional, and sets up the named testimonial section that must follow.

## Long copy: should Capita use it?

Yes — unambiguously, yes. Ogilvy: "the more facts you tell, the more you sell." Capita is selling a $45,000–$500,000+ engagement to a C-suite buyer who is making a high-consideration purchase. The current site is a brochure. The buyer needs a dossier.

**Where it goes:** A long-form section titled "Why we publish prices" — positioned between the packages and the FAQ. This is the founder letter Ogilvy used in B2B accounts where credibility was the whole sale.

**What it says — first 250 words verbatim:**

---

**Why we publish prices, and why we are advisory only.**

For most of the ten years Capita has existed, we did not publish prices and we did not productise our work. Every engagement was a bespoke retainer, scoped to the client, priced in a back-and-forth that took two to six weeks before the work could begin.

We are changing that for one reason. Boards and executive teams have told us, repeatedly, that the cost of figuring out what an advisory engagement costs — three meetings, four follow-ups, an internal procurement cycle — is itself a tax on getting the work done. If you are a Chief Customer Officer with a quarterly board commitment to move retention by two hundred basis points, the last thing you need is a six-week procurement process to hire the advisory firm that's going to help you do it.

So we are publishing the four engagements we are asked for most often, with their inclusions, their durations, and their prices, on the page above this one. Roughly seven in ten of the conversations we have begin with one of those four shapes. The other three in ten are bespoke — usually a board-led programme, a multi-business-unit operating-model redesign, or an AI governance build for a regulated entity — and we continue to scope those on a retainer basis. Bespoke engagements start at $120,000 and are quoted within five business days.

We are advisory only. We do not build, we do not implement, we do not resell, we do not partner with systems integrators for revenue share. Our independence is the product.

---

**Proof elements that accompany the long copy:**
- A photograph of the principal(s), captioned with their full name, title, the company they ran before founding Capita, and one specific result.
- A list of the last twelve engagements by industry, problem, and the named outcome metric — even if the client is anonymised at this point.
- Two named testimonials inset into the long copy, formatted with photo, name, title, company.
- A signature: the principal's actual signature in image form, beneath the letter. Ogilvy used this pattern in the Schweppes Commander Whitehead campaign and the Hathaway eye-patch campaign. It works because it claims authorship and personal accountability.

## Packaged-options pivot — Ogilvy treatment

### How Ogilvy would lay out a price list for considered B2B advisory

- **Anchor highest first.** Ogilvy's price-list logic in the Rolls-Royce campaign and the Mercedes campaigns leaned on the high anchor doing the heavy lifting — readers process the top number first, then perceive everything below it as comparatively reasonable.
- **Call them by what they produce, not what happens inside them.** "Operating Model Build" beats "Strategy Sprint." Buyers buy outcomes.
- **Use "from" pricing for one reason only: it is honest.** If the package can run longer or wider, "from $X" with the duration band is correct. "Starting at" or "investment of" is consultancy euphemism — strike it.
- **What's included must be specific.** Ogilvy: facts sell. "Two executive workshops, one architecture review, one written operating-model document signed by the principal" beats "workshops and a deliverable."

### The bespoke route — how Ogilvy frames it

The error to avoid: making "bespoke" sound premium and the packages sound like the discount tier. Ogilvy would say both routes serve real buyers and the copy should make the bespoke route's audience self-identify without making the package buyer feel they bought the consolation prize.

**Verbatim bespoke section copy:**

---

**Bespoke advisory**
*For boards running programmes the four packages above do not cover.*

Roughly three in ten of our engagements do not fit the four shapes above. They tend to be one of three things:

- A board-led programme spanning multiple business units, where the operating-model question is bigger than one practice.
- A multi-year customer or AI transformation already underway, where the question is "are we doing this right" and the brief needs an independent review.
- A regulated-entity engagement where governance, AI evaluation, and operating-model questions sit together and need to be built from scratch.

For these, we work on retainer. The engagement principal sits on your steering committee for the duration. Bespoke retainers start at $120,000 and are scoped within five business days.

## Top 10 highest-leverage rewrites

1. **Hero H1.** Current: "Turn AI, data, and customers into a durable growth system." → Proposed: "Capita Technology now publishes packaged advisory, with prices. Bespoke continues by request." **Rule:** Lead with news. (Ogilvy: never bury news.)

2. **Trusted-by section.** Current: Eight unnamed logos. → Proposed: Three named, titled, photographed testimonials. **Rule:** Named testimonials sell. Anonymous logos signal weakness.

3. **Primary CTA.** Current: "Request a working session." → Proposed: "See the four packages and prices." **Rule:** Tell the reader what they get, not what happens.

4. **Method H2.** Current: "Four moves that turn AI and customer programs into compounding growth." → Proposed: "Four engagements. Each one ends with a written plan your teams ship against." **Rule:** Specifics sell; superlatives like "compounding" without proof do not.

5. **Outcomes H2.** Current: "Clarity that only ex-operators can deliver." → Proposed: "Three outcomes our last forty engagements have moved: retention, revenue, and AI delivery." **Rule:** Facts beat adjectives. The number 40 is a fact; "clarity" is an adjective.

6. **FAQ section header.** Current: "Questions we hear most often." → Proposed: "What boards ask us before they sign." **Rule:** Reframe FAQ as buyer-objection handling; flag the audience explicitly.

7. **Contact H2.** Current: "Share the context. We respond with advisory next steps." → Proposed: "Tell us the number you need to move in the next 90 days. We reply within two business days with a scoped proposal and a price." **Rule:** Specific commitments outsell vague invitations.

8. **Add: Founder letter section.** Currently absent. → Proposed: Signed long-copy founder letter between packages and FAQ. **Rule:** Long copy works for considered B2B purchase. Signature claims authorship.

9. **Add: Named guarantee.** Currently absent. → Proposed: "Our 90-day commitment" section after the packages. **Rule:** Ogilvy's risk-reversal principle. Reduces buyer friction at the moment of decision.

## What Ogilvy would refuse

- **The unnamed logo wall.** Ogilvy: "If your client won't let you name them, you don't have a case study, you have a rumour." Strike it.
- **The kinetic-phrase visual interruption.** Repeating "Operator-grade clarity" twice as a "visual interruption" is the kind of art-direction flourish Ogilvy associated with awards-show advertising. The product is not the hero in a kinetic-typography moment. The promise is.
- **"Compounding growth" and "compound lifetime value."** Borrowed VC vernacular. C-suite buyers in regulated industries do not say "compound" in this context. Strike both instances.
- **The eyebrow "AI · Data · Customer Growth."** Keyword-soup eyebrows belong on agency portfolio sites. Strike.
- **"Request a working session" and "Request intro."** Hedge language. The buyer wants to know whether to talk to you or buy from you. Tell them.
- **"AI-powered decision systems" in the meta description.** Jargon stack. Strike "AI-powered" as a modifier — it adds nothing and triggers buyer skepticism.
- **The textarea placeholder "Tell us about the outcome you want to move in the next 90 days."** It is good copy — but as a placeholder it implies the field is optional. Make this a labelled, required field with the same wording.
- **"Independent executive advisory" repeated without proof.** Saying you are independent without listing the vendors you are independent of is unverified self-praise. Either prove it (see the named non-affiliations list above) or stop claiming it.
- **The phrase "decision artifacts."** Internal language. Buyers do not search "decision artifact" and do not say it in board meetings. The word is "document" or "plan."
- **"What's on your mind?" as a form label.** Reads like a therapist intake form. The form should ask the question the engagement begins with: "What's the number you need to move and what's your deadline?"
