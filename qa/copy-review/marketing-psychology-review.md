# Marketing Psychology Review — Capita Technology

## Frame

A C-level executive landing here is risk-averse, status-conscious, and time-poor. They've been burned by big-name consultancies that overpromised and by AI vendors who sold capability without operating reality. They are afraid of two specific losses: spending a six-figure budget on a deck nobody executes (regret aversion + sunk-cost anticipation), and being the person who bet on AI without governance and got named in a post-mortem (loss aversion). They are biased toward known names, named operators, and recent vivid evidence. The strongest psychological lever this site is currently under-using is **availability heuristic combined with authority bias**: there are no named operators, no specific named outcomes, no recent vivid stories — so the page reads as competent but ungrounded, which the executive brain stores as "polite generalist." The packaged-pricing pivot is also a missed opportunity for **anchoring + decoy + price relativity**, all of which would make the bespoke engagement feel obviously reasonable rather than obviously expensive.

## Packaged-options pivot — psychological architecture

**Number of packages: Three.**
Rationale: Paradox of Choice + Price Relativity / Good-Better-Best. Three is the canonical centre-stage configuration — it creates an anchor (top), a decoy or default (middle), and a low-friction entry (bottom). Four packages would dilute the centre-stage and force the executive to compare across two dimensions (scope and price). Two packages have no middle and force a binary that triggers regret aversion.

**Ordering and visual emphasis:**
Display left-to-right: lowest price → middle (centre-stage, visually emphasised, "Most chosen" or "Recommended for first engagements" tag) → highest price.
Centre-stage bias makes the middle package the default. Anchoring from the right (highest price) makes the middle look reasonable. The lowest package serves as the entry door (foot-in-the-door) and also as a contrast option that prevents the middle from looking like the cheap choice.

**Pricing presentation:**
Show prices bare and round, in AUD, *no decimals, no "from," no "starting at."* Hide nothing behind a click.
Principle: Ambiguity aversion + rounded-price fluency effect. Senior executives reading an advisory site will read "Contact for pricing" as "expensive and arbitrary" — that is the default consultancy frame the productisation is designed to escape. Round numbers (e.g. $35,000, $75,000, $150,000) signal premium and are easier to process than charm prices ($34,995) which read as B2C and damage authority. Bespoke remains "scoped on request" — the contrast between fixed-price packages and bespoke "scoped" reinforces that bespoke is for genuinely custom work, not a hidden upsell.

**Package naming convention:**

Three-package naming options (pick one set):

Option A — Artifact-named (recommended): `Diagnostic` · `Roadmap` · `Program`
- Psychology: each name *is* the artifact the buyer receives. Availability heuristic does its work — the executive can visualise what arrives. Names also map cleanly to a procurement line item.

Option B — Time-named: `Two-Week Diagnostic` · `90-Day Roadmap` · `Quarter-by-Quarter Program`
- Psychology: time-bounded names neutralise the open-ended consultancy fear. Goal-gradient is built into the names.

Option C — Outcome-named: `Find the Number` · `Move the Number` · `Govern the Number`
- Psychology: jobs-to-be-done at its purest. Strongest emotional pull but slightly less procurement-friendly.

**Four-package naming options** (if four is required for SEO or comparison-table reasons):
`Diagnostic` · `Roadmap` · `Program` · `Board Advisory (retainer)`
- Adds a fourth retainer-style tier above the program. Retainer is mechanically different (ongoing) and does not violate centre-stage because the program remains the default; the retainer functions as an anchor at the high end.

Recommendation: use Option A names with Option B timing as the subhead.

**The "or talk to us" bespoke escape hatch:**

**Position**: a single full-width band *immediately beneath the three package cards*, visually quieter than the packages (no card background, no price chip, single line of copy + one CTA). It must not compete with the packages for centre-stage but must be unmissable for the executive who has scrolled past the cards thinking "none of these fit."

Body: `Some engagements are larger, longer, or genuinely custom — multi-business-unit transformations, regulator-driven remediation, AI-program governance for listed companies. Tell us what you are trying to move and we will scope and quote in writing.`
CTA: `Request a bespoke scope`

**Why this position serves the prospect psychologically**:
- Status-quo bias and default effect: the packages are the default path. The bespoke option is visually demoted, which means the visitor who *would have* contacted us anyway is funnelled to a package — and the visitor who genuinely needs custom work still finds the door.
- Door-in-the-face inversion: by showing the fixed-price packages *first*, the bespoke quote arrives in the prospect's head anchored against $150k rather than against an open consultancy budget. This makes a $300k bespoke quote feel referenced rather than arbitrary.
- Pratfall: explicitly naming what does *not* fit ("multi-business-unit transformations, regulator-driven remediation") shows the advisor knows where the line is. Authority bias rewards this.

### Risk-reversal copy

Place a single risk-reversal line at the bottom of the packages section, in small caps or eyebrow weight, spanning all three cards:

`Every packaged engagement carries a written success contract. If, at the end of the diagnostic phase, you do not believe the work changes how you would brief the program, we end the engagement and bill only the Diagnostic.`

Rationale: a full money-back guarantee would damage authority (high-end advisors do not refund) and is not believable for a senior advisory product. The "end the engagement after diagnostic" reversal is psychologically equivalent for the regret-averse executive: the maximum loss is bounded at the lowest-priced package ($35k), regardless of which package they purchase. This converts the perceived risk of a $150k Program from "$150k at risk" to "$35k at risk" without changing the price.

## Anti-recommendations

For a C-level / SVP advisory audience, the following moves — common on lower-end B2B SaaS pages — will *destroy* trust and should be explicitly avoided:

1. **Fake scarcity timers or "3 spots left this month" widgets.** Senior buyers have seen these on growth-marketing pages and will read them as desperation. If availability is genuinely tight, say so in plain language once (see "Availability / cadence signal" above). If not, say nothing.

2. **Charm pricing on packages ($49,995, $74,997).** Reads as B2C and damages the premium frame. Use round numbers. The rounded-price fluency effect is doing real work for premium positioning.

3. **Aggressive social-proof counts ("Trusted by 500+ executives," "100+ engagements delivered").** Boutique advisory has the opposite scale story to tell. Volume signals dilution to this audience. Lean on industry and specificity instead.

4. **Anonymous quotes ("CEO, Fortune 500 telco said…").** Read as fabricated. Use named operators with permission, or use the structural-naming pattern ("Engagements have spanned three of the four major Australian banks") which is provable through bios.

5. **Money-back guarantees on the full engagement price.** Damages authority. Senior advisors do not refund; they re-scope. The asymmetric "stop at the diagnostic" reversal does the same psychological work without breaking the frame.

6. **Free downloadable lead magnets gated by a long form ("Download our AI Maturity Whitepaper — fill 9 fields").** High activation energy for an audience that already has more whitepapers than time. If a sample artifact is offered, ungate it. Reciprocity only fires when the gift is genuinely free.

7. **Live chat widgets, calendar embeds in the hero, or any "Book a 15-minute discovery call" CTA.** Executives do not book themselves. Their EAs do, and EAs need a form trail with context for filing. Keep the form.

8. **Emoji, exclamation marks, gradient hero illustrations, animated counters of "$ saved."** Each one moves the page one notch toward the founder/marketer register and away from the advisory register. The current site is correctly calibrated; the productisation should not change the register.

9. **Customer logos at full opacity competing with the hero.** The current muted treatment is correct. Authority signals should be present but not loud — senior readers will look for them; they do not need them shouted.

10. **"As featured in" press logos unless they are tier-1 mastheads with bylined commentary by the founder.** A list of Medium publications damages authority. If the founder has been quoted in the AFR, *Banking Day*, or the equivalent regulator-side trade press, name those specifically with the article title; otherwise omit.
