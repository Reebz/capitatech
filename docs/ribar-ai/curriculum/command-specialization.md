# Ribar.ai — Command specialization curriculum (weeks 5–12)

Command is the strategic AI leadership track. The end-of-track deliverable is **an AI strategy + governance framework, presented (rehearsed in this program) to the exec's actual board**, with a sequenced transformation plan beneath it.

Eight modules, one per week. The cadence is week-of-thinking with weekly 1:1 with Mitch acting as senior strategy partner. The exec brings the seat; the program brings the structure.

---

## Module M1 (week 5) — Where the exec stands today

**Learning outcome:** The exec has a written current-state map — what their business does with AI today, what the gaps are, what the board is asking for, what the team is shipping (or not).

**Key concepts:**

- Current-state audit framework: capabilities, investments, talent, vendors, governance.
- The four common postures: AI-skeptic, AI-curious, AI-active, AI-strategic.
- Mapping who-owns-what across the org.
- The gap between what the board thinks AI is doing vs. what it actually is doing.
- Spotting the AI work hidden in non-AI departments.

**Session anchor question:** *If your board asked tomorrow "what is our AI capability today" — what would the honest one-page answer be?*

**Exec deliverable:** Current-state map for AI in the exec's organisation. One page; named accountabilities; honest about gaps.

---

## Module M2 (week 6) — Strategy formation: the three-horizon view

**Learning outcome:** The exec has drafted a three-horizon AI strategy — what is shipping in 12 months (H1), what is being built in 12–36 months (H2), and what is being researched for 3+ years out (H3).

**Key concepts:**

- The McKinsey three-horizon model applied to AI.
- Why most AI strategies fail by living only at H1 (immediate features) or only at H3 (theatrical "AI vision").
- Linking horizons: how H2 work depends on H1 capability-building.
- The board-friendly format: one slide, three horizons, named owners, named investments.
- Common strategy traps: AI as a strategy vs. AI in service of a strategy.

**Session anchor question:** *What is your business strategy, and where does AI either enable it, threaten it, or change it entirely?*

**Exec deliverable:** Three-horizon AI strategy doc, draft v1.

---

## Module M3 (week 7) — Architecture and platform decisions

**Learning outcome:** The exec understands the platform-level decisions their organisation has either made or is avoiding — and can defend a position on each.

**Key concepts:**

- Build / buy / partner decisions at AI scale.
- Foundation-model choice and the implication of vendor lock-in.
- Data platform readiness for AI (and how to spot the unready state).
- Talent topology: where AI lives in the org structure (centralised AI team vs. embedded vs. centre-of-excellence).
- Architecture decisions that compound: the 3–5 calls that affect everything downstream.

**Session anchor question:** *What is one platform decision your team is avoiding because it is too political or too expensive to make right now?*

**Exec deliverable:** Architecture decision log: the 5–8 calls the organisation has made or needs to make, with the exec's recommended position on each.

---

## Module M4 (week 8) — Governance, risk, and the regulator

**Learning outcome:** The exec has drafted the AI governance framework appropriate to their regulatory and risk context — what is governed, by whom, on what cadence, against what indicators.

**Key concepts:**

- Governance scope: data governance, model governance, output governance, vendor governance, employee-use governance.
- Risk frameworks: NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act (where applicable), APRA / ASIC / ACMA expectations (where applicable).
- Evaluation and audit: how the organisation proves the AI it ships is doing what it claims.
- The role of the audit committee, risk committee, and board on AI.
- Operational governance: who can ship an AI feature, who approves, who monitors.

**Session anchor question:** *If your regulator walked in next month and asked how you govern AI, what would you show them?*

**Exec deliverable:** AI governance framework v1: governance scope, accountability, evaluation cadence, escalation paths.

---

## Module M5 (week 9) — Transformation sequencing

**Learning outcome:** The exec has a sequenced 12-month transformation plan — what ships when, who owns each milestone, what depends on what.

**Key concepts:**

- Sequencing principles: build platform before features; build governance before scale; build talent before architecture.
- Dependency mapping at programme level.
- Quick wins vs. compounding investments — when each is the right call.
- The "make work / display work" problem: theatre projects vs. real value.
- Programme cadence: monthly, quarterly, annual rhythms.

**Session anchor question:** *If you could only deliver three of the AI initiatives your team is considering in the next 12 months, which three and why?*

**Exec deliverable:** 12-month sequenced transformation plan. Named owners. Named milestones. Named dependencies.

---

## Module M6 (week 10) — Talent, structure, and the operating model

**Learning outcome:** The exec has decided how AI capability will live in the organisation — centralised, embedded, federated, or hybrid — and has a 6-month talent plan.

**Key concepts:**

- The four common topologies: central AI team, embedded AI engineers in each function, centre of excellence, hybrid.
- The build-vs-buy decision for AI talent.
- Reskilling existing talent vs. hiring new talent.
- Vendor + partner ecosystem: who you work with, how that affects the operating model.
- The first three hires (or first three role changes) that compound.

**Session anchor question:** *What is the one role change in your org that would most accelerate AI capability — and why is it not happening?*

**Exec deliverable:** Talent + operating model plan: structure, first hires, reskilling priorities, vendor partnerships.

---

## Module M7 (week 11) — Board engagement and rehearsal

**Learning outcome:** The exec is ready to present their AI strategy + governance framework to the board. They have rehearsed against Mitch and incorporated feedback.

**Key concepts:**

- Board-deck structure for an AI strategy presentation: 6–10 slides, three-horizon framing, governance framing, risk framing, financial framing.
- Anticipating board questions: the seven questions a board will ask, and the honest answers.
- Voice in the room: confidence vs. certainty (the latter is the trap).
- Handling the AI-skeptic director and the AI-enthusiast director in the same room.
- What to NOT show the board (drafts, internal debates, vendor pitch slides).

**Session anchor question:** *What is the one thing your board would push back hardest on — and how do you want to handle it?*

**Exec deliverable:** Board deck v2 (after rehearsal feedback). Rehearsed once with Mitch in this session; rehearsed a second time before the actual board meeting.

---

## Module M8 (week 12) — Post-board reflection and forward plan

**Learning outcome:** The exec has presented to the board, captured the outcome, and committed to a 90-days-from-now operating plan.

**Key concepts:**

- Post-board capture: what got approved, what got pushed back, what surprised.
- Translating board outcomes into the next 90 days of operational moves.
- The 90-day check-in: what indicators define "the strategy is working."
- Optional graduation path: ongoing monthly retainer, return for the next strategy cycle, or step out clean.
- What the exec carries forward as their permanent operating model.

**Session anchor question:** *Six months from now, what is the one decision you will look back on as the one that mattered most?*

**Exec deliverable:** Board outcome captured + 90-day forward operating plan + indicators for the 90-day check-in.

---

## End-of-track deliverables

By the end of week 12, the Command graduate has:

1. **AI strategy** — three-horizon, board-approved (or at least board-presented), with named owners and milestones.
2. **AI governance framework** — scope, accountability, evaluation cadence, escalation paths.
3. **12-month transformation plan** — sequenced, dependency-mapped.
4. **Talent + operating model plan** — structure, hires, partnerships.
5. **A board presentation delivered** — rehearsed in the program, presented in the real room.
