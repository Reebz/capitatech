# Ribar.ai — Catalyst specialization curriculum (weeks 5–12)

Catalyst is the personal-activation track. The end-of-track deliverable is a **personal AI playbook** + an **active daily practice** the exec is running. Eight modules, one per week. Each module builds on the personal AI workflow finalised in Foundation week 4.

---

## Module C1 (week 5) — Personal decision drafting with AI

**Learning outcome:** The exec uses AI to draft and pressure-test executive decisions — not to replace judgment, but to surface counterarguments and structure ahead of meetings.

**Key concepts:**

- Decision-prep prompts: "Here is the call I am about to make; what is the strongest argument against it?"
- Counterarguer pattern vs. brainstormer pattern.
- Structured decision templates (ICE, DACI, MOSCOW) as prompt scaffolds.
- Time-bounded vs. open-ended decisions.

**Session anchor question:** *What is the next non-trivial decision on your calendar — and how would AI-assisted prep change how you walked into the room?*

**Exec deliverable:** One real upcoming decision drafted twice — once unaided, once with AI prep. Notes on what changed.

---

## Module C2 (week 6) — Daily AI rituals

**Learning outcome:** The exec runs at least three daily AI rituals on autopilot — morning briefing, end-of-day capture, calendar prep — without thinking about whether to do them.

**Key concepts:**

- Morning briefing: digest of overnight emails, calendar, agenda.
- End-of-day capture: what got decided, what got deferred, what got dropped.
- Calendar prep: pre-meeting briefs (what is this meeting about; who is in the room; what is the outcome).
- Voice dictation into AI for capture-heavy moments.

**Session anchor question:** *Which three rituals would compound the most if you ran them daily for thirty days?*

**Exec deliverable:** Three rituals defined, scheduled, and operating. Capture the prompt patterns used.

---

## Module C3 (week 7) — Email and writing leverage

**Learning outcome:** The exec uses AI to multiply their writing throughput while keeping their own voice. Drafts come from them; AI accelerates them.

**Key concepts:**

- Voice calibration: training Claude on the exec's writing samples.
- The "draft → refine → ship" cycle for emails, memos, board updates.
- When to write from scratch and when to draft-with-AI (the line is finer than people think).
- Pitfalls: AI sycophancy, over-formality, losing edge.

**Session anchor question:** *What is one weekly written artifact that, if you halved the time to produce it, you would write better and ship more often?*

**Exec deliverable:** Three pieces of writing the exec has shipped this week, each annotated: which AI helped, what they changed manually, what they would do differently.

---

## Module C4 (week 8) — Meeting leverage

**Learning outcome:** The exec uses AI to extract more from every meeting they sit in — pre-brief, in-session capture, post-meeting synthesis.

**Key concepts:**

- Pre-brief patterns: what to ask about a meeting before walking in.
- In-session note-taking and AI summarisation (Otter, Granola, Claude Projects, etc.).
- Post-meeting synthesis: turn raw notes into commitments, owners, dates.
- Confidentiality and tool selection for sensitive meetings.

**Session anchor question:** *What is the meeting on your calendar this week where post-meeting follow-through is weakest — and what would you change?*

**Exec deliverable:** A defined meeting workflow used on at least three meetings this week. Output: a synthesis doc per meeting; commitments tracked.

---

## Module C5 (week 9) — Personal research and synthesis

**Learning outcome:** The exec uses AI for executive-level research — competitive intelligence, market sizing, regulatory scans — at a speed that changes how often they do it.

**Key concepts:**

- Research patterns: starting from a question, not from a tool.
- Iterative refinement vs. single deep-dive.
- Source skepticism: how to ask AI for citations and verify them.
- Document synthesis from PDFs, decks, and long-form articles.
- When to use search-augmented vs. closed-context AI.

**Session anchor question:** *What is one research question you have been delaying because the time investment is too high — and what would change if it took ten minutes?*

**Exec deliverable:** One executive-quality research brief produced in this week, on a question the exec actually has. Document the prompt sequence.

---

## Module C6 (week 10) — Personal AI for self-management

**Learning outcome:** The exec uses AI to manage their own time, energy, and priorities — not as a productivity gimmick, but as an executive function support.

**Key concepts:**

- Quarterly + weekly + daily planning patterns with AI as thought partner.
- Energy management: AI-assisted reflection on when the exec does best work.
- Priority pressure-testing: "Tell me what I should drop from this list."
- Reading and learning capture: turning newsletters / podcasts / books into compounding notes.

**Session anchor question:** *If you had a personal chief of staff for thirty minutes a day, what would they ask you and what would you ask them?*

**Exec deliverable:** A defined self-management workflow used over the week. Outputs: weekly review, energy notes, dropped-priority list.

---

## Module C7 (week 11) — Privacy, security, and tool selection

**Learning outcome:** The exec can confidently decide which AI tools to use for which classes of information — without needing to delegate the decision.

**Key concepts:**

- Data classification at executive level (public, internal, restricted, regulated).
- AI tool risk dimensions: data retention, training opt-out, geographic processing.
- Working with the corporate IT / security policy (and when to push back).
- Personal vs. corporate AI accounts and how to keep them clean.
- Quick framework for evaluating a new AI tool.

**Session anchor question:** *What is one piece of information you have refused to put into AI so far — and is the risk you are managing the actual risk?*

**Exec deliverable:** Personal tool-stack decision log: which AI tools, used for what, with what data classifications, and a one-line risk note per tool.

---

## Module C8 (week 12) — Playbook finalisation and forward plan

**Learning outcome:** The exec finalises their personal AI playbook and commits to the next-90-days practice they will run after the program ends.

**Key concepts:**

- Playbook structure: rituals, prompts, tools, capture cadence, review cycle.
- Sustainability: what will the exec actually keep doing after week 12?
- 90-day forward plan: three things to expand, two things to drop.
- Optional graduation path: Construct bolt-on, monthly retainer, or full step-out.
- The 90-day check-in: indicators, scheduling, what triggers the no-fee follow-up week.

**Session anchor question:** *Three months from now, what does your personal AI practice look like — and what do you want me to ask you about it then?*

**Exec deliverable:** The final personal AI playbook + a signed 90-day forward plan. Indicators for the 90-day check-in defined. Optional graduation path declared (Construct bolt-on / monthly retainer / step out clean).

---

## End-of-track deliverables

By the end of week 12, the Catalyst graduate has:

1. **Personal AI playbook** — rituals, prompts, tool-stack, capture cadence, review cycle. ~10–15 pages.
2. **Active daily practice** — three or more rituals operating without prompts from Mitch.
3. **90-day forward plan** — what to keep, what to expand, what to drop. Signed.
4. **Indicators for the 90-day check-in** — what "the practice worked" looks like in measurable terms.
