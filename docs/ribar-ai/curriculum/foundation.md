# Ribar.ai — Foundation curriculum (4 weeks, shared across all tracks)

Every client starts here, regardless of track. Foundation adapts to the exec's starting level via 1:1: a Claude Code user goes deep on edge cases of the same topics; a beginner spends more session time on vocabulary and tool mechanics. Same outcomes, different depth.

The Foundation deliverable for week 4 is a **personal AI vocabulary + workflow doc** the exec authors during weeks 1–3 and finalises with Mitch in week 4. It is the exec's reference for the rest of the program.

---

## Module F1 — Mental models and vocabulary

**Learning outcome:** The exec can hold a fluent conversation about AI capabilities, limits, and failure modes without resorting to vendor pitch language.

**Key concepts:**

- Generative vs. discriminative AI; foundation models vs. fine-tuned models; pretrained vs. instruction-tuned.
- Tokens, context windows, and why "summarise this PDF" is sometimes hard.
- Hallucination, grounding, and retrieval-augmented generation in one sentence each.
- The cost-quality-latency triangle.
- The difference between an LLM, an agent, an assistant, and a copilot.

**Session anchor question:** *When someone in your seat says "we should use AI for this," what do you now ask them to clarify before agreeing?*

**Exec deliverable for the week:** A one-page personal glossary of the 12–15 AI terms the exec will encounter most often in their role, written in their own words.

---

## Module F2 — Claude and core tool usage

**Learning outcome:** The exec is comfortable using Claude (or equivalent) as a daily working tool — knows how to prompt, how to evaluate output, and how to integrate into their existing workflow.

**Key concepts:**

- Prompting fundamentals: context, role, constraints, examples.
- Iterative prompting vs. one-shot prompting.
- When to use Claude in a chat surface vs. embedded in another tool.
- Project / Workspace organization (Claude Projects, custom GPTs analog).
- Voice + tone calibration so output matches the exec's register.

**Session anchor question:** *What is one task you do every week that you have not yet tried with Claude — and why?*

**Exec deliverable for the week:** Three prompts the exec has used successfully on their own work, each annotated with what worked and what they would change next time.

---

## Module F3 — Common pitfalls and what AI is not

**Learning outcome:** The exec can spot the three or four most common ways AI implementations fail before they fail, and the four or five claims vendors make that should trigger skepticism.

**Key concepts:**

- Hallucination patterns and how to design around them.
- "AI for X" projects that are really "data cleanup for X" in disguise.
- Why model accuracy benchmarks rarely predict real-world performance.
- Vendor red flags: vague capabilities claims, no evaluation discipline, no human-in-the-loop story.
- The gap between demo and production.

**Session anchor question:** *What is one AI claim you have heard in the last month that you now want to revisit?*

**Exec deliverable for the week:** A short written critique of one real AI claim the exec has seen (pitch, vendor demo, internal slide) — what would they ask now that they would not have asked before?

---

## Module F4 — Personal AI workflow setup

**Learning outcome:** The exec has a defined personal AI workflow they will run during the specialization weeks: which tools, when, for what, and how they capture what works.

**Key concepts:**

- Daily AI rituals: morning briefing, decision drafts, calendar prep.
- Calendar and email integration patterns.
- Note-taking and knowledge-base patterns (Notion / Obsidian / Apple Notes + AI).
- Voice-to-AI pipelines (dictation in, draft out).
- Capture-and-review discipline: what worked, what did not, weekly review.

**Session anchor question:** *What does your week look like if you use AI for three things you do not currently use it for — and which three are highest-leverage to start with?*

**Exec deliverable for the week:** The personal AI workflow doc — finalised. This is the artifact carried into specialization weeks. It captures: tools used, daily/weekly rituals, capture cadence, and one explicit "what I will try this week" experiment.

---

## How Foundation adapts to skill level

**Beginner arrival:** Sessions weight 60–70% to F1 (mental models / vocabulary) and F2 (tool fluency). F3 and F4 still happen but as lighter passes.

**Intermediate arrival:** Sessions weight 30% to F1 (refresh and gap-fill), 30% to F2 (advanced prompting, Projects, multi-turn patterns), and 40% to F3 + F4 (pitfalls, workflow design).

**Advanced arrival (Claude Code user, has shipped AI features):** F1 and F2 collapse into a single session covering edge cases and second-order topics (model evaluation patterns, agent design considerations). F3 and F4 expand to take the full remaining time, oriented toward leadership-level architecture and workflow design.

Mitch makes the calibration call after the kickoff diagnostic.

---

## Foundation completion criteria

By end of week 4, the exec can:

1. Hold a 15-minute conversation with their CTO or AI lead about an AI initiative WITHOUT either party using vague or vendor language.
2. Demonstrate at least three Claude prompts they use on their own work.
3. Identify, in writing, two things AI is currently doing well in their domain and two things AI is not yet ready for.
4. Operate a defined personal AI workflow they will run during weeks 5–12.

If any of the four criteria are not met, week 5 starts with a one-session Foundation extension to close the gap. The specialization start may slip by one week; the 12-week container remains the price commitment.
