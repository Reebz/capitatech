# Ribar.ai outreach — warm intro template

Length target: under 250 words. First-person. Should sound like a real note between two people who already know each other — because it is.

Warmer than cold by a measurable margin. The recipient already trusts Mitch's professional credibility at some level. Lead with the shared history briefly; pivot to the program; ask for 30 minutes.

---

## LinkedIn DM version

> [First name],
>
> Been a while. [One-sentence reference to your last interaction or a known development in their world. Examples:
> — "I saw you moved into the [role] at [company] — congratulations. I imagine [specific thing] is interesting from that seat."
> — "We crossed paths last at [event / project] — I think [year]?"
> — "Thinking about [shared project history] earlier this week — that work still holds up." ]
>
> Quick update on me: after Optus, [whatever your most recent step the recipient might not know about — running Capita Technology, building Ribar.ai, etc.], I am now running a 1:1 AI executive program for forty-plus senior leaders. Twelve weeks, three tracks — Catalyst for personal AI activation, Construct for shipping AI-built tools, Command for strategic AI leadership. Founder-led, no junior coaches, no platform.
>
> Reason I am writing: I am opening a founding cohort — ten clients, by direct invitation only. I thought of you because [specific reason — their current role, a specific AI activation problem you know they have, the type of work they are doing now]. If one of the tracks lines up with what is on your desk, I would value a 30-minute conversation to see whether the fit is real.
>
> Site is at [ribar.ai], calendar is here [Cal.com link]. If neither works, just reply and we will sort it.
>
> Hope you are well.
>
> — Mitch

---

## Notes on what makes this warm vs. cold

- "Been a while" or "thinking about [shared history]" — establishes the prior relationship as the context, not a cold outreach with a relationship as the hook.
- "I thought of you because [specific reason]" — must be REAL. If you cannot complete that sentence with a specific reason, this is not a warm outreach; it is a cold one. Send the cold template instead.
- The close ("hope you are well") signals familiarity. Use only if you know them well enough that it does not read as performative.

## Notes on what NOT to do

- Do NOT use the warm template for someone you barely worked with. The mismatch reads as fake-familiarity. Use the cold template.
- Do NOT bring up old grievances ("we never quite finished that project") even if true. Save those conversations for the discovery call if relevant.
- Do NOT skip the program description because "they already know you." The audience may know your credibility but not your current product. Spell it out.

## Reply paths

Identical to the cold template, with one variant:

- **Positive reply with a specific question about prior shared history:** answer briefly, then redirect to the discovery call. Do not let the program conversation slide into a catch-up that delays the qualifying conversation.
