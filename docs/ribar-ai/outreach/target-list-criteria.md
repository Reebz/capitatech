# Ribar.ai — founding-cohort target-list criteria

Defines who Mitch reaches out to for the founding cohort. The plan does NOT include the actual list (that is user-owned PII managed in Notion); this file defines the criteria so the list can be populated by Mitch directly.

The founding cohort goal: first ten clients OR end of Q3 2026, whichever comes first. The discount (60% off Ladder A) buys testimonials. Wrong targets dilute the testimonial value and burn the discount.

---

## Persona — the founding-cohort client

### Demographic

- **Age:** 40+ (the brand promise is to forty-plus leaders; younger execs are not disqualified but are not the target).
- **Seat:** Senior executive, functional leader, or independent operator with C-suite-equivalent authority. Examples: CMO, CFO, CHRO, COO, CIO, CTO, CDO, business-unit head, NED with AI portfolio, principal of a boutique consulting / advisory firm.
- **Geography:** Australia / New Zealand / Singapore / Hong Kong / SEA / occasional UK or US referrals. Sydney-based targets allow in-person discovery; remote targets are also fine (delivery is video by default).
- **Industry:** No hard restriction, but Mitch's prior credibility is highest in telco, banking, financial services, insurance, SaaS, property, and subscription businesses. AI/ML/data businesses themselves are weaker fits (they have internal capability).

### Behavioural

- **Has authority to commit the founding-cohort price** ($10k Catalyst / $20k Construct / $40k Command) without lengthy procurement.
- **Has the time** to commit to twelve weekly 1:1 sessions of ~60 minutes plus prep.
- **Has a stated AI activation problem** they have not yet solved — either personal (Catalyst), build (Construct), or strategic (Command).
- **Will be a credible name on a testimonial** — public-facing role, recognisable title, willing to be associated with Ribar.ai marketing.

### Disqualifiers (do NOT add to target list)

- Anyone Mitch would NOT want as a public testimonial. Reputational risk applies in both directions.
- Existing direct competitors of Capita Technology (would create an awkward Capita / Ribar.ai overlap).
- Vendors trying to sell into Mitch (the founding cohort is not a sales-channel introduction).
- People whose AI activation problem is not real — they are signing up out of curiosity, not commitment.
- People who would expect special access, custom curriculum, or board observer status as part of the engagement.
- Anyone who has previously had a contentious working relationship with Mitch (the program is twelve weeks of close work; pre-existing friction will surface).
- Anyone with active legal or regulatory issues attached to their name (testimonial risk).

## Sourcing channels (in priority order)

### 1. 1st-degree LinkedIn connections

Highest-yield. Mitch has direct credibility, so the cold message is warm by default. Filter by:
- Job title contains C-level keywords or "Director" + function name.
- Location: AU, NZ, SG, HK, UK, US.
- Mutual connections strong (≥5 shared connections in priority).
- Active on LinkedIn in the last 90 days (signals reachability).

Target volume: 30–60 names per outreach wave.

### 2. MIT Sloan alumni network

The MIT credential is the leverage. Filter the Sloan alumni directory by:
- Industry match (telco / banking / financial services / insurance / SaaS).
- Geography match.
- Class year approximately ±10 years from Mitch's class (peer-group recognition).
- Active in alumni events or LinkedIn (signals openness to peer outreach).

Target volume: 20–30 names per outreach wave.

### 3. Past Optus / JPMC / Brand Networks / Quantium / PwC colleagues

Direct working-relationship credibility. Filter by:
- Current C-level or function-head seat.
- Worked with Mitch in a substantive capacity (not "same building, never spoke").
- No active commercial conflict (not currently competing with Capita).

Target volume: 15–25 names per outreach wave.

### 4. Conference / event attendees

If Mitch attends a relevant conference (RAFA, AICon, FST Banking, Telco Transformation, etc.), in-person meetings convert at higher rates than cold. Add anyone who shows AI activation interest in the conversation.

Target volume: 5–10 names per event.

### 5. Inbound from `ribar.ai` (when site is live)

People who land on the site via founder-letter referral, podcast appearance, or other indirect channel. Higher self-selection than outbound, but lower volume initially.

### 6. Referrals from Capita Advisory clients

If a Capita client has the personal AI activation problem, they may self-identify or Mitch may flag the fit. Founding cohort referrals from existing trusted clients are the highest-quality channel.

## Disqualifying questions for the discovery call

The discovery call exists to surface disqualifiers before either party commits. Mitch should ask:

1. *What is the AI activation problem you want this program to address?*
   - If the answer is generic ("I want to learn AI"), disqualify or downgrade to Catalyst at most.
2. *What does success look like for you at week 12?*
   - If the answer is vague, surface the gap before signing.
3. *What is your authority to commit budget and twelve weeks of your calendar?*
   - If the answer requires multi-party approval, the founding-cohort discount may close before procurement clears. Document explicitly.
4. *Are you comfortable being a public testimonial after the program?*
   - If the answer is anything other than a clear yes, do NOT offer founding-cohort pricing. Offer Ladder A or decline.
5. *Is there anything in your current professional context (regulatory, legal, reputational) that would make a testimonial complicated?*
   - If there is, do NOT offer founding-cohort pricing.

## Volume targets

- Founding cohort size: 10.
- Discovery-call conversion (assume): 1 in 5 reached out → 20% (a generous starting estimate for personal-brand outreach to qualified targets).
- Discovery-call-to-signed conversion (assume): 1 in 3.
- To sign 10, target ~150 outreaches across 4–6 waves over Q2–Q3 2026.

These numbers are assumptions; calibrate after the first wave's actual conversion data.
