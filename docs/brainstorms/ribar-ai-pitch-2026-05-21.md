## Brand 2: Ribar.ai

### Architecture
- New brand. Personal-brand modern-handle naming. Likely domain: `ribar.ai` (or similar short modern handle — confirm during ce-plan).
- Direct-to-executive motion, premium B2C-flavored pricing.

### Audience
40+ executives, cross-functional, feeling left behind by AI but framed in the brand as activators-in-waiting. Cross-industry. Likely buyer types:
- Functional leaders (CMO, CFO, CHRO, COO) whose roles are being reshaped by AI
- Direct AI owners (CIO, CTO, CDO) wanting outside perspective
- Boards / NEDs developing AI literacy
- Career-transition execs building a personal AI thesis

### Positioning (layered, AIDA-aligned)
| Layer | Function | Message |
|---|---|---|
| Top (Attention) | Operator + Credential | "MIT-trained operator who built production AI at JPMorgan Chase and Optus." |
| Middle (Interest/Desire) | Emotional | "I'm 40+, I've navigated this, I'll guide you. The 'left behind' fear is real; here's the way through." |
| Bottom (Action) | Structure | Three tracks revealed at pricing/package selection — not at the headline. |

### Program structure
- **Format:** 1:1 only
- **Container:** 12 weeks per track
- **Decomposition:** 4-week shared Foundation + 8-week track-specific Specialization
- **Cadence:** Weekly 1:1 sessions, ~1 hour each, light curriculum between sessions
- **Post-program:** Optional ongoing support (e.g. monthly 90-min retainers)
- **Community:** Opt-in community for graduates once client volume justifies (avoid empty-room launch)

### The three tracks
| Track | Outcome | Specialization focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Catalyst** | Personal AI activation | Daily AI fluency, personal productivity, individual practice. The exec becomes their own catalyst. |
| **Construct** | AI-powered building | Builds apps/tools with Claude Code, hands-on as a maker. Output is tangible AI-enabled work product. |
| **Command** | Strategic AI leadership | AI architecture, transformation, board engagement, big-picture leadership of an AI agenda. |

### Foundation (shared, 4 weeks)
Same across all three tracks. Adapts to exec's starting level via 1:1 delivery (depth scales — a Claude Code user goes deep with Mitch; a beginner gets onboarded gently). Coverage:
- Vocabulary and mental models for AI
- Core tool usage (Claude, prompts, LLM mechanics)
- Common pitfalls — what AI is and isn't
- Personal AI workflow setup

### Pricing — list (Ladder A, top-of-market)
| Product | Duration | List price |
|---|---|---|
| Catalyst | 12w | $25k |
| Construct | 12w | $50k |
| Command | 12w | $100k |
| Catalyst → Construct bolt-on | 8w | $30k |
| Construct → Command bolt-on | 8w | $60k |

Bolt-ons priced at ~60% of full track. Graduates skip the 4-week Foundation; the discount also incentivizes graduation. Bolt-ons have higher effective hourly rate than full tracks — that's the LTV compounding.

### Founding cohort offer
- **Scope:** First 10 clients OR through end of Q3 2026, whichever comes first
- **Discount:** 60% off list price on full tracks → Catalyst $10k / Construct $20k / Command $40k
- **Bolt-ons:** Excluded from founding discount; graduates pay full bolt-on price. Preserves LTV economics.
- **Required exchange (contractual):** Written, named testimonial within 60 days of program completion, with title, company, and right to use in Ribar.ai marketing materials. Soft asks rarely deliver; this is in the statement of work.
- **Framing language:** "Founding cohort pricing" — never "discount." Anchor protection.
- **Distribution:** Outreach-only to a vetted target list. Not publicized on the website.

### Annual revenue band (post-founding, at Ladder A list)
- Capacity: 6 concurrent × 4 cohort turns/year = 24 engagements/year max
- Realistic with marketing/admin/vacation: 15–18 engagements/year
- Revenue band: **$675k–$1.08M/year**
- LTV compounding: as graduates accumulate, mix shifts toward bolt-ons (higher hourly margin)

### Time investment (working assumptions)
| Engagement | Mitch-hours |
|---|---|
| Full track (12w) | ~25h (12 sessions + 6h prep + 3h kickoff/wrap + ~4h async) |
| Bolt-on (8w) | ~16h (8 sessions + 4h prep + 1h kickoff/wrap + ~3h async) |

### Effective hourly rate (Ladder A list)
| Engagement | Price | Mitch-hours | $/h |
|---|---|---|---|
| Catalyst | $25k | 25h | $1,000 |
| Construct | $50k | 25h | $2,000 |
| Command | $100k | 25h | $4,000 |
| Bolt-on Construct | $30k | 16h | $1,875 |
| Bolt-on Command | $60k | 16h | $3,750 |

Founding-cohort effective hourly is ~40% of these (60% off). Acceptable trade for testimonials.

## Cross-brand strategy

### Why two brands, not one
The two buyers are psychologically different:
- **Capita Advisory buyer** is solving a TEAM/ORG problem (retention bleeding, AI program stalled, operating model broken). They hire to fix their team.
- **Ribar.ai buyer** is solving a PERSONAL problem (feeling left behind, role being reshaped, want to lead AI). They hire to fix themselves.

Same person could be both at different moments, but mixing the offerings on one site dilutes both pitches. Separate brands let each buyer see only their lane.

### What the brands share
- Mitch Ribar bio (MIT MBA + JPMC + Optus + Brand Networks)
- Founder identity
- Operator-credential anchor

### What the brands DON'T share
- Audience and pricing
- Visual identity (Ribar.ai = modern tech-flavored handle; Capita = enterprise advisory register)
- Sales motion (Capita = considered B2B with proposals; Ribar.ai = direct-to-exec with published pricing and founding-cohort outreach)

### Cross-link strategy (deferred to ce-plan)
- Subtle bio-level cross-reference on each site
- Not aggressive cross-sell — let buyers self-route
- Capita Advisory site bio addendum: "Mitch also runs Ribar.ai, a 12-week AI executive program for senior leaders."
- Ribar.ai bio addendum: "Mitch is principal of Capita Technology, an independent advisory for telco / banking / insurance / SaaS leadership teams."
