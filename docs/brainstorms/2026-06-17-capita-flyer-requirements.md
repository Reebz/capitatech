Date: 2026-06-17
Status: convergent brainstorm (build-ready requirements; not final marketing copy)
Format: A4 portrait, single side. Print + on-screen PDF leave-behind for senior executives.
Owns: the slots, the architecture, proof selection, skeleton copy.
Does NOT own: the final headline, or the final five method-step names. Those are `[HEADLINE PLACEHOLDER]` and `[METHOD STEP NAMES PLACEHOLDER]`, delivered by the parallel ideate pass.

---

## 1. Goal + the single reader takeaway

**Goal:** in one A4 face, prove that Capita Technology owns a rare intersection — AI, customer value, and strategy — and that one operator has actually run all three at enterprise scale, so a senior buyer keeps the page and emails the principal directly.

**The single thing a CFO remembers after ten minutes:** *"One independent advisor has actually run AI, customer growth, and strategy at the scale of my business — and I have his direct line."* Not a method. Not a price. A person at an intersection nobody else can honestly claim.

Everything on the page serves that one memory. The intersection states the territory; the CV proves it is earned, not asserted; the method shows how the work is done; the contact makes acting trivial.

## 2. Page architecture — single A4 face, top to bottom

Ordering logic (Ogilvy: lead with the strongest claim, anchor highest-value first, earn the eye before asking for anything). The reader's eye is bought in this order: **brand → headline (the intersection promise) → the proof it is true (the three circles + the operator CV) → how the work runs (method) → what you can buy (tiny engagement list) → how to reach him (contact).** Proof comes before method because at a leave-behind glance, *who he is* outsells *how he works*; the method is the third differentiator, not the opener.

Reserve "half the Fortune 100" (Brand Networks) and "10x SaaS revenue growth" as optional swap-ins if a slot reads thin — both are safe. ## 5. Print / layout constraints

**Page + safe area**
- A4 portrait: 210 × 297 mm. Design at 300 DPI for print export (2480 × 3508 px).
- Outer margin / safe text area: 12–15 mm on all sides. Keep all type and the contact details inside this — losing the principal's email to a printer's edge-clip is the one unacceptable failure.
- Full-bleed blue bands (hero, contact) extend to **216 × 303 mm with 3 mm bleed** past the 210 × 297 trim on all four sides; add crop marks for any commercial print. For office/PDF use, full-bleed-to-trim is fine.

**Type sizes for A4 (print points, not the site's responsive clamps — the clamps are viewport-based and must be pinned for print):**
- H1 hero: 34–44 pt (Geist, weight 600). Big enough to be the poster, small enough to fit a real headline on 2–3 lines.
- Section labels / eyebrows: 8.5–9.5 pt mono, uppercase, 0.05em tracking, `--muted-strong` for AA.
- Body / proof lines: 9.5–10.5 pt Inter.
- Method step names: 13–15 pt Geist; numerals 16–20 pt mono Capita blue.
- Contact email/mobile: 11–13 pt Geist Mono (must stay easily readable / transcribable).
- Triad circle labels: 10–12 pt mono.
- Minimum any text on the page: 8 pt. Nothing smaller, including the engagement list.

**Color / ink for the full-bleed blue (founder chose exact web match):**
- Screen/PDF: use sRGB `#0078f0` (blue), `#d946ef` (magenta), `#f6f6f3` (warm-gray), `#0b0b0d` (ink), white.
- For commercial CMYK print, `#0078f0` will shift (it sits outside CMYK gamut and prints duller/greener). Provide a CMYK proof and accept the shift, or supply a Pantone match if brand fidelity matters at press. Flag this — the founder's "exact web match" is achievable on screen/RGB PDF but not guaranteed on a CMYK press.
- Heavy full-bleed blue uses a lot of toner on a desktop printer. Acceptable for a premium leave-behind; note it.

**B&W office-printer degradation (must survive this):**
- The blue hero band prints as mid-dark gray; **white headline type on it must stay legible in grayscale** — verify contrast holds when blue → ~50% gray.
- Magenta marker block and magenta rules drop to gray; ensure the headline still parses without the color cue (the marker must not be the only thing carrying meaning).
- The three-circle figure must read in pure outline/line-weight, not rely on fill color to separate the lenses — use distinct strokes + labels so it survives B&W.
- All contact details are ink-on-white or white-on-dark — both survive grayscale. Keep them out of any color-only treatment.

**HTML build — `@media print` vs screen:**
- Build as one HTML page; the on-screen view and the print/PDF view are the same single A4 face (this is a flyer, not a long-scroll site — no responsive sections to collapse).
- `@media print`: set `@page { size: A4 portrait; margin: 0; }`, force `-webkit-print-color-adjust: exact; print-color-adjust: exact;` so the full-bleed blue bands actually render in the PDF, pin all `clamp()` font sizes to fixed `pt` values, disable the ASCII grid's cursor-driven spotlight (freeze to the static tint), and remove any hover/focus chrome.
- `@media screen`: render at A4 aspect in a centred frame so on-screen preview matches the print exactly.
- The ASCII spotlight grid: on print, render as the static faint tint only (no animation, no JS spotlight). Keep it subtle enough not to muddy the proof text.

## 6. Open risks / things the build must get right

1. **CMYK color shift on the blue** — "exact web match" holds for screen/RGB PDF, not a CMYK press. Decide screen-PDF-only vs press, and if press, supply a Pantone/CMYK proof. (Flagged; needs a founder call.)
2. **Three-circle device is the single highest AI-slop risk on the page.** A gradient Venn reads instantly as a SaaS diagram. It must be flat, line-led, editorially restrained, and survive B&W. Get this wrong and the whole "not generic consultancy" positioning collapses.
3. **Numeral discipline** — numerals 1–5 only on the method steps. No decorative numerals anywhere else (no "20+" rendered as a giant figure, no "100M" as hero ornament). The scale facts use numerals inline in body text, which is fine, but nothing numeric becomes a decorative display element outside the method.
4. **Contact details must be real and verified before any print run** — the email and mobile are placeholders here; a printed flyer with a wrong number is unrecoverable. Confirm exact `mitch@…` address and mobile.
5. **No prices anywhere** — the live website publishes prices; the flyer brief forbids them. The build must not inherit price strings from the site's package data. The engagement list is names only.
6. **One face only** — if content overflows, cut a scale fact or shorten the method clauses before spilling to a second side. The "fits one face" budget assumes the headline stays ≤ ~10 words and method clauses stay to one short line each.
7. **Marker-block + magenta as meaning-carriers** — anything the magenta is doing must also be legible without color (B&W and color-blind safety).

## Recommended rendering of the three-circle intersection (for print, non-SaaS)

Do **not** draw three overlapping translucent gradient circles — that is the SaaS-diagram tell. Instead:

**A flat three-lens triad, line-led, with a named centre token.**
- Three circles as **thin ink strokes only** (matching the site's `--chunky-border` weight idiom, ~2 pt), no fills or only a 4–6% warm-gray wash. Arranged in the standard triad (two top, one bottom, or one top + two bottom).
- Each lens labelled in **Geist Mono small-caps** at its outer edge: `AI`, `CUSTOMER VALUE`, `STRATEGY` — labels sit *outside* the circles so the overlap stays clean.
- The central overlap region (where all three meet) is the **only filled element**: a small solid Capita-blue lozenge or disc, ringed in magenta (the page's two brand colors meeting exactly where the three disciplines meet — the color does the argument). Inside or beside it, a 2–4 word mono label: `THE INTERSECTION` / `WHERE THE WORK LIVES`.
- One line beneath the figure carries the argument in words so the figure never has to stand alone: *"Most advisors hold one. The value is where all three meet."*
- **B&W survival:** because separation comes from line strokes + outside labels (not fill color), and the centre token is a filled shape (reads as dark in grayscale) ringed by a distinct stroke, the whole figure parses in pure black-and-white. The magenta ring degrades to a gray ring but the centre still reads as the focal point.

This keeps the device firmly in the website's editorial-poster idiom: ink line-work, mono labels, the two saturated brand colors used as a precise accent at the one meaningful point — not a decorative gradient cloud.
